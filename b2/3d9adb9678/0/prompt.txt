## Structure

Four acts, one continuous recording of me talking, one break to full screen.

| Act | Content | Budget | Frame treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Me + my background | ~15s | On camera. Lower-third with name and credential. |
| 2 | What I am building | ~15s | On camera. Kinetic phrase overlays on key lines. |
| 3 | How Haven works | ~40s | Full-screen product film. My footage video is suppressed, my audio keeps running underneath. |
| 4 | The vision | ~15s | Back on camera. Closing statement overlay. |

Act 3 is the bulk of the work and the part investors care about.
It shows exactly four UI beats: the sky, search, a person card, and the reveal.
Four beats landing cleanly beats six beats blurring past.

The UI does not exist as shippable software yet, so build it as fake HTML screens that match `mvp-design.md` and the real SwiftUI structure.
It must look like the product will actually look, because it is a promise I intend to keep.

## Hard pipeline order

```
write script -> record on camera -> transcribe for word timings -> build act 3 -> build overlays -> composite -> render MP4
```

The transcript is the master clock.
Every overlay hangs off word-level timings from my actual recorded take.

Do not animate anything before a recorded take exists.
If you find yourself building motion with no transcript in hand, stop and ask me for the recording.

## Technical decisions already made

- **HyperFrames**, not Remotion. Not Claude Design (it is a static design-system surface and cannot animate).
- Compositing happens inside HyperFrames. My footage goes into the composition; overlays and act 3 render on top. One MP4 out, no external editor.
- 16:9, 60-90 seconds total.
- Start from the `/hyperframes` entry-point skill and let it route. `talking-head-recut` is the likely owning workflow. Load `motion-doctrine` before authoring any motion.

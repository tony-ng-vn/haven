What you are describing is not initially a **relationship-depth map**. It is a **personal acquaintance graph**:

> Given the communication data available to me, show me everyone in my social world and how those people appear to be connected to one another.

That is a much cleaner abstraction.

## The messages should disappear

The user does not fundamentally care about viewing 74 group chats or thousands of messages. Those are the raw observations used to construct the map.

The transformation is:

```text
Messages + Contacts
        ↓
Identify unique people
        ↓
Observe shared conversations
        ↓
Infer who likely knows whom
        ↓
Display a map of people
```

So in the final abstraction:

* **People are the visible objects.**
* **Connections between people are the visible structure.**
* **Group chats are hidden evidence explaining those connections.**
* **Messages are mostly implementation detail.**

A group chat should only become visible when someone asks, “Why do you think these two people know each other?”

## The user is implicit

Because everyone on the map is someone **you know**, drawing a line from you to every person adds no information. It produces a giant starburst.

You do not necessarily need to appear as a node at all.

The entire canvas is already “your world.” The meaningful connections are the relationships among everyone else:

```text

          \        /
           \      /
             Maya

  Jordan ───── Casey ───── Taylor
```

The product is showing:

* Who belongs to the same social neighborhoods
* Who connects otherwise separate neighborhoods
* Who appears to know many people in your world
* How one person is connected to another

That is the topology.

## The rings in the current image may be answering the wrong question

“Inner circle,” “close friends,” “friends,” and “familiar” introduce relationship depth and emotional hierarchy.

But you just said depth is not the point.

So I would remove those rings, or at least not make them the primary structure. Instead, physical position should mean:

> People are near one another because they share conversations, contacts, and mutual connections.

The result would look less like a solar system ranked around you and more like a geographic map containing different social regions.

For example:

```text
        COLLEGE                         WORK

       Kit                           Omar
             Quinn ─────────── Jordan

                  BASKETBALL

             Leo   Pax   Cameron
```

Quinn and Jordan might sit between regions because they connect multiple groups. Their position itself tells you something without requiring every line to be shown.

## All people should be present, but not all information

This is the key visual abstraction:

> **Show every person, but do not show every label and every connection simultaneously.**

At the full-map level:

* Every person exists as a dot.
* Social regions appear as clouds, territories, or constellations.
* Only major region names and a few important people are labeled.
* Most connection lines are hidden.
* Bridge people can be subtly emphasized.

When you hover or select a person:

* Everyone else fades slightly.
* Their likely acquaintances become visible.
* Relevant lines appear.
* Shared groups can be revealed as evidence.

When you select two people:

* Show whether they are connected.
* Show their shortest connection path.
* Explain which chats or mutual people support the connection.

So the data model can remain completely flat—people and edges—while the interface uses semantic zoom to manage scale.

## Group chats are actually hyperedges

There is one technical subtlety that matters a lot.

Suppose 30 people are in the same group chat. It would be misleading to automatically draw every pair as knowing each other. That creates 435 person-to-person connections from one chat, even though some members may barely recognize one another.

Underneath, the truthful structure is:

```text
Person A ─┐
Person B ─┤
Person C ─┼── Group Chat
Person D ─┤
Person E ─┘
```

A group chat is a shared room connecting many people at once. It is not automatically proof that every participant has a meaningful connection with every other participant.

The system can convert shared rooms into **connection confidence**:

* Two people in one large chat: weak evidence
* Two people in several small chats: stronger evidence
* They reply or react to each other repeatedly: stronger evidence
* They appear together over several years: stronger evidence
* User confirms they know each other: confirmed edge

So the visible graph might distinguish:

```text
Solid line     Confirmed or strongly observed connection
Soft line      Likely connection
No line        No evidence available
```

Importantly, no line would mean “we have not observed a connection,” not “these people definitely do not know each other.”

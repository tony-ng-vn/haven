product

## Platform

web

## Users

People who meet a lot of others over a life -- through work, events, travel, friends of friends -- and who want to stay connected instead of letting those relationships quietly lapse.
The defining moment of use is recall: you half-remember someone (a name fragment, where you met, one detail) and you want to find your way back to them so you can reach out.
There is one audience, the person keeping their own memory layer. Nothing here is shared, social, or multi-user; every person and note is private to the owner.
Success looks like a user opening Haven, finding the person they were reaching for, and leaving to actually reconnect.

## Product Purpose

Haven is a personal memory layer over the people of your life. Social media and contacts help with the first connect, not with maintaining a relationship over time; Haven is that maintenance layer.
Its one job is to help you find back the people you have met, from whatever fragment of memory you bring, so you can keep the connection alive.
The core loop is small on purpose: search or add a person, add a link and whatever context you want to remember, save, and return to search. The value is the quiet handoff at the end -- the right person, given back.

## Positioning

Bring a fragment of memory; Haven gives you back the person. It is the one place built to refind the people you have met, not to collect, rank, or broadcast them.

## Brand Personality

Calm, focused, content-first. The voice is warm, plain, and unhurried -- it talks like a person, never like a product demanding attention.
The feeling to leave a user with is quiet relief: the small, grounding moment of being handed back someone you had half-forgotten. Nothing urgent, nothing loud, nothing that performs.

## Anti-references

Not a social network: no feeds, likes, follower counts, activity streams, or engagement mechanics. Haven is private and quiet, not something you perform in.
Not a CRM or sales tool: no pipelines, deal stages, lead scoring, or dashboards of metrics. These people are relationships, not leads to work.

## Design Principles

Hand back the person, not a dashboard. Every screen earns its place only if it helps return a person from a fragment of memory. The search box and the person are the heroes; everything else stays quiet.

No scoring, no judgment. Haven never ranks closeness, grades how well you know someone, or nags you to reconnect before you lose them. It holds memory; it does not keep score. Recency (when added, when last touched) exists only to keep the screen useful, never as a health signal.

The tool disappears into the task. One primary action per screen. Content leads, chrome recedes. Familiar, trustworthy affordances over invented ones, so the interface never asks the user to stop and figure it out.

Quiet by default. Restraint in color, motion, and copy. Depth and softness (gentle shadows, large radii, spring-like transitions) carry the feel, not decoration or noise. Motion conveys state, never demands attention.

Private and personal. This is one person's memory layer. Nothing is shared or observed; the design should always feel like a private space, not a network.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text holds at least 4.5:1 contrast, interactive controls show a visible focus state, and touch or click targets stay large and forgiving.
Respect the system: follow light and dark preference, and honor prefers-reduced-motion with a calm non-animated fallback for every transition.

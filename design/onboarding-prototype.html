What I changed, and the one place the platform still says no

Yes, and it doubles as the photo importer

1. Authorize to connect. Typing is the exception, pasting is the fallback.

The redirect-and-authorize flow you keep drawing is real and you already own the plumbing: Clerk supports X, LinkedIn, Facebook and more as one-tap social connections. Tap a platform, it bounces out to authorize, you come back connected.

What this mock does: tapping a row waits three quarters of a second and fills in a fixed demo handle. On device that pause is the real round trip out to the app and back. The prototype cannot perform an actual redirect; the shipped app will.

The screen is now split by input model, because that is the honest division:

  • Connect an account - every social row. All three say Connect and all three start by authorizing. None of them advertise a link field.
  • Or type one - phone, where there is no account to authorize against, so typing is the correct primary path rather than a compromise.

Cut to four: LinkedIn, X, Instagram, phone. What comes back from an authorization differs by platform, so the flow degrades in steps, and each step only becomes visible after the one above it fails:

  • X returns the handle with the token. You tap once and nothing else appears.
  • LinkedIn signs you in and verifies you, but never sends your profile address. It prefills a best guess and asks you to check it, so it is a confirm rather than an entry.
  • Instagram connects for Creator and Business accounts. A personal account is the one case that lands on a paste field, with a line saying whose limit it is. Paste a full URL and we strip the handle out of it.
  • Phone is typed, because there is no account to authorize against.

The rule the screen encodes: a link field is never something a platform offers, only something an attempt falls back to. If Instagram loosens its rules tomorrow, that row loses its field and nothing else changes.

The upside you may not have expected: every successful authorization returns a profile photo as well. That is why removing the photo question in note 6 costs you nothing.

4. Intelligent search, not just character matching

Both, layered. Literal matching stays as the instant floor. On top sits the semantic layer we discussed: type who did I meet about design or creative people and it returns the right faces even when those exact words are nowhere in the record, with a small line showing how it read your question. In the mock the ranking is faked; in the app it is embeddings over the capture notes, which is the whole reason capture records context and not just a handle.

Try a plain word like figma for literal, then a phrase like the founder from the dinner for the smart layer.

6. Photo is out of onboarding

Gone. Onboarding is three questions: name, city, contact. The photo has two better moments to arrive - it comes in free when you connect an account (note 1), and you can add or change it later in your profile. When you save someone else, the same import pulls their photo. No moment in the flow asks you to stop and pick an image.

The part worth arguing about

Authorization proves who someone is. It does not hand you a link to them.

You drew a line between reading through an API and the sign-in-with-Facebook flow. They are the same machinery: the redirect returns a token, and what that token can tell you is set by the provider. The flow itself is genuinely easy, and Clerk does it in a line, so building it is not the problem.

The problem is the specific thing you want at the end of it. You said it plainly: as long as we can get the user profile so we can redirect people there with the QR code. That is the one thing an authorization reliably will not give you.

  • LinkedIn's identifier is pairwise, which means app-scoped. Every app that signs you in receives a different opaque string for you, and none of them can be turned into a public profile address.
  • Facebook is the same shape, which is part of why it is cut.
  • X does return the username, but reading it sits behind paid API tiers.
  • Instagram returns it for Professional accounts only.

So a perfect authorization flow still leaves you unable to construct the link in most cases. Worth knowing before it gets built rather than after.

The way out is that the beacon should never have pointed at Instagram

Point it at an address you own. The beacon on screen 08 now resolves to inhavens.com/tony, and that one change dissolves the problem:

  • No platform sits between two people connecting, so no API decision can revoke the feature.
  • Whoever scans it lands on a page showing every way to reach you, not just the one platform you happened to pick.
  • It works when they do not have Haven yet, which is the only version of this loop that functions at zero adoption.
  • You see the connection happen, which is the whole of Phase 2.

Under that model a handle stops being the destination and becomes content on the destination. The API limits stop blocking the handshake and only affect how much someone types when adding a handle, which is the smaller problem the Connect flow already solves.

Authorization still earns its place, just for a different job than the one you had in mind: verification. Connecting a real Instagram proves the handle on a Haven page belongs to that person rather than being something they typed. Worth having. Not what unblocks the QR code.
